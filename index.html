Getting the smart scale to tell you the truth

Consumer impedance scales are poor at absolute body fat — a validated multi-frequency device still read men 4.2 percentage points low versus DXA — but excellent at tracking change (test–retest ICC 0.987–0.995). That only holds if conditions are identical each time:

  • Same time of day — first thing in the morning
  • After the toilet, before food or drink
  • Bare feet, dry — damp soles read differently
  • Not after training, sauna, or alcohol — both shift body water badly
  • Weekly, not daily — the day-to-day noise is bigger than the real change

Treat the number's direction as real and its value as approximate. Bone mass barely moves over months and is largely derived from the same model — log it if you like, but don't let it drive decisions.

How this connects to the rest

Your daily totals write straight into the Today tab's calorie and protein fields, so the protein-shortfall and lean-mass flags run off what you actually ate rather than a guess. Scanned products are cached on your phone the first time you look them up — after that they work with no signal.

On accuracy: the built-in library holds reference values per 100 g. Real products vary, and cooking changes weight substantially — 100 g of dry buckwheat becomes roughly 300 g cooked. Weigh food in the state you log it, and edit any library item whose numbers don't match your packet.

The five things that decide this

  • 150 g protein a day. Most commonly missed, most consequential.
  • Three resistance sessions a week, for years. The difference between losing 20 kg and losing 20 kg well.
  • Alcohol. Highest-leverage single change in your profile.
  • 7–8 hours of sleep. Decides what tissue you lose, not just how you feel.
  • A deficit you can hold for a year, not one you can survive for six weeks.

Food, adapted to what you actually eat

  • Build on: tvorog, kefir, Greek yoghurt, eggs, chicken and turkey breast, white fish, unsalted oily fish, lentils and beans, grechka, barley, oats, rye bread, cabbage, beetroot, carrot, tomato, cucumber, olive oil, walnuts.
  • Cut back hard: salted and smoked fish and meats, salo, kolbasa, pickled vegetables, mayonnaise salads, white bread and pastries, pelmeni in quantity, sweetened drinks and compote, salt at the table.
  • Easy swap: Greek yoghurt for mayonnaise in Olivier — about 280 kcal off a serving, and it works better than you'd expect.
  • Alcohol: zero during the loss phase is the strongest recommendation. If not, cap at 3–4 drinks a week, never more than 2 in a sitting, always with food.

Not medical advice. Targets are model estimates (Mifflin–St Jeor with activity multipliers and adaptive recalculation); individual energy requirements vary ±10–15%. Adjust from your own observed rate of change over the first 3–4 weeks rather than trusting the formula. Blood pressure falls as weight falls — if you take antihypertensive medication, monitor at home and keep your doctor informed, since doses often need reducing. Discuss the training plan with a clinician before starting, particularly given reported joint and back issues.
